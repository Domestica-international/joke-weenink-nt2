Civic Integration in the Netherlands: everything about the exam and your preparation

You live in the Netherlands and you need to integrate (inburgeren). But what does that actually involve? Which exams do you need to pass? And how do you prepare as well as possible? In this article I explain everything step by step.

What is civic integration (inburgering)?

Civic integration means learning to participate in Dutch society. You learn the language. You learn how the Netherlands works. And you show that you're ready to live, work and thrive here.

The government requires many newcomers to integrate. You get a certain amount of time for this. Usually three years. Within that period you must pass the civic integration exam.

Civic integration is not just an obligation. It's also an opportunity. Because when you speak the language, everything becomes easier. You can understand your neighbours. You can help your children at school. And you truly feel at home. Read more about why learning Dutch is so important.

Who needs to integrate?

Not everyone needs to integrate. The rules depend on your situation. Generally: if you come to the Netherlands from outside the EU and want to stay, you need to integrate.

  • Asylum status holders (people with an asylum residence permit)
  • Family migrants (partner or child of someone in the Netherlands)
  • Spiritual leaders (imams, priests, etc.)

You can get an exemption if you already have a Dutch diploma, are under 16, or above the state pension age. EU expats are not required to integrate, but increasingly choose to learn Dutch anyway.

The parts of the civic integration exam

The civic integration exam consists of six parts. You must pass them all. Each part tests a different aspect of your language skills or knowledge of the Netherlands.

1. Reading

You read short texts and answer questions. Think of letters, emails, advertisements and news items. The level is A2. That means: simple, everyday texts.

2. Listening

You listen to conversations, announcements and messages. Then you answer questions. It's about everyday situations: at the doctor's, at work, in the supermarket.

3. Writing

You write short texts. For example, an email to school, a note for your neighbour or filling in a form. The spelling doesn't need to be perfect. The point is that your message is clear.

4. Speaking

You repeat sentences and answer questions about pictures and situations. Many students find this part nerve-wracking. But with good NT2 lessons you practise until you can do it with confidence.

5. Knowledge of Dutch Society (KNM)

This exam is not about language, but about the Netherlands itself. How does health insurance work? What are your rights as an employee? How does education work? You learn about history, geography, norms and values.

6. Orientation on the Dutch Labour Market (ONA)

With the ONA you show that you understand how the job market works. You create a portfolio with your work experience, diplomas and job application skills. This part has no exam but a final interview.

What level do you need?

For the language parts (reading, listening, writing, speaking) you need A2 level. That's basic level. You can manage in simple, daily situations. To reach A2 you need approximately 120 lesson hours. View the full overview of lesson types and levels.

How to prepare for the civic integration exam?

Good preparation is half the battle. You have several options. The most important: choose a Dutch language course that suits you.

Option 1: Language school

At a language school you sit in a group of 15 to 25 students. The advantage: structure and a fixed schedule. The disadvantage: little personal attention. If you don't understand something, the lesson just continues.

Option 2: Private lessons with an NT2 teacher

With an experienced NT2 teacher you get all the attention. The lesson is adapted to your pace and your weak points. This is the fastest way to pass your exam.

Option 3: Small group lessons

This is the best of both worlds. You learn together with others, but the group is small enough for personal guidance. With Joke there are maximum 6 students per group. That makes a big difference. Read more about the NT2 course options.

Option 4: Online lessons

Convenient if you have a busy schedule or don't live nearby. Online lessons via video work well. You have the same interaction as in the classroom. And you save travel time.

How long does preparation take?

That depends on your starting level, your mother tongue and how much time you invest. On average:

  • A1 level: approximately 80 lesson hours
  • A2 level (civic integration): approximately 120 lesson hours
  • B1 level: approximately 200 lesson hours

Most of Joke's students pass within 40 weeks. That's less than a year. And the pass rate? 98%. That's not a coincidence. It comes from small groups, personal attention and a clear lesson plan.

Practical tips for your civic integration

After more than 21 years as an NT2 teacher I know exactly what works. Here are my best tips:

  1. Start early. Don't wait until the last moment. The sooner you start, the more relaxed the journey.
  2. Practise every day. Better 20 minutes per day than 3 hours on Saturday. Regular practice works better.
  3. Speak Dutch outside lessons. Talk to your neighbours, order in Dutch, listen to Dutch podcasts.
  4. Don't be afraid to make mistakes. Mistakes are normal. They help you learn. Nobody laughs at you.
  5. Choose the right teacher. A good teacher makes the difference between passing and failing. Look at the pass rate and group size.
  6. Make a plan. Know when you want to take your exam. Work towards it. A clear goal keeps you motivated.

Failing is annoying, but it's not the end of the world. You can retake the exam. There are extra costs though. That's why good preparation is so important.
