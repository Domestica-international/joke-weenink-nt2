NT2 Explained

Dutch as a Second Language (NT2): what is it and how does it work?

By Joke Weenink · NT2 teacher · 21+ years of experience

You've moved to the Netherlands. Now you want to learn the language. But where do you start? In this article I explain what Dutch as a Second Language (NT2) actually is. You'll learn which levels exist, what the difference is with NT1 and how the education system works.

I'm Joke Weenink, NT2 teacher with more than 21 years of experience. In that time I've helped over 1,000 students. From beginners to advanced learners. From expats to newcomers who needed to pass their civic integration exam.

What is NT2?

NT2 stands for Nederlands als Tweede Taal (Dutch as a Second Language). It means you're learning Dutch while it's not your mother tongue. You grew up with a different language. Now you're adding Dutch.

The difference with NT1 is simple. NT1 means Dutch as a First Language. Those are people who grew up with Dutch as their mother tongue. They learned the language as children, at home and at school.

With NT2 you learn Dutch in a different way. You already have a language. Now you're adding Dutch to it. That requires a different approach. A good NT2 teacher knows exactly how that works.

The CEFR levels explained

In Europe we use the CEFR (Common European Framework of Reference for Languages). It divides language proficiency into levels. For NT2 there are four important levels.

A1 - Starter

You can introduce yourself and answer simple questions. You understand short sentences about familiar topics. Think of doing groceries or asking for directions.

A2 - Basic user

You can manage in daily situations. You can have a conversation at the doctor's, at school or at work. This is the level for the civic integration exam.

B1 - Independent

You can participate in conversations about work, study and leisure. You understand the main points of clear texts. You write coherent pieces.

B2 - Advanced

You communicate fluently with native speakers. You understand complex texts and can express your opinion on various topics. This level is needed for the state exam NT2.

Each level builds on the previous one. You start at A1 and work your way up step by step. The speed depends on your background, motivation and how much you practise.

How much time does each level take?

Average study time

You can reach A1 level in about 80 lesson hours. For A2 you need around 120 lesson hours. With private NT2 lessons it often goes faster. In a group you practise more with other students. Both have their advantages.

These hours are a guideline. Some students learn faster. Others need more time. That's completely normal. What matters is that you keep practising.

Who takes NT2 education?

NT2 is for everyone who didn't grow up with Dutch. In practice, these are often these groups:

  • Newcomers who need to integrate and want to pass the civic integration exam
  • Expats who work in the Netherlands and want to speak the language (read more in expats in the Netherlands)
  • Partners of Dutch people who want to participate in daily life
  • International students who want to stay in the Netherlands after their studies
  • Employees who need Dutch for their job or promotion

Each group has different goals. That's why personal education is so important. A good Dutch language course takes your situation into account.

NT2 and civic integration

Many people learn Dutch because they need to integrate. Since 2022 the new Civic Integration Act applies. The municipality helps you with a personal plan. But you need to do the work yourself.

For the civic integration exam you need at least A2 level. The exam tests four skills: reading, listening, writing and speaking. In addition, you take the Knowledge of Dutch Society (KNM) exam.

With good NT2 lessons you prepare specifically for each part. You don't just practise the language. You also learn how the exam works. So you walk in with confidence.

How does NT2 education work in the Netherlands?

You have several options to learn Dutch. The three most common are:

1. Private lessons

You get lessons from a teacher, just for you. The programme is completely customised. This is the fastest way to progress. Ideal if you have specific goals or want quick results.

2. Group lessons

You learn together with other students. Usually in a small group of maximum 6 people. You practise a lot with each other. That's great for your speaking skills.

3. Online lessons

You take lessons via video call, wherever you are. Flexible and effective. Perfect if you have a busy schedule or don't live nearby. Read more about the options in our article on comparing NT2 courses.

Which form suits you best depends on your situation. Check the prices to get an idea of the costs.

What to look for when choosing NT2 education?

Not every language school is the same. Pay attention to these points:

  • Teacher experience. How many years has the teacher been teaching? What results do students achieve?
  • Small groups. In a small group you get more attention. Maximum 6 people is ideal.
  • Personal approach. Is your level and goals taken into account?
  • Pass rate. How often do students pass their exam?
  • Flexibility. Can you catch up on lessons or take them online?
