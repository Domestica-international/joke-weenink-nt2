NT2 lessons: everything you need to know before you start

By Joke Weenink · 1,000+ students guided

You've decided to learn Dutch. Good plan. But where do you start? NT2 lessons come in many forms. Private, group, online, in-person, for civic integration or for work. How do you choose?

In this article I explain everything. What NT2 lessons are exactly. What types exist. What they cost. And how to find the best lessons for you.

What are NT2 lessons?

NT2 stands for Dutch as a Second Language. NT2 lessons are lessons specifically designed for people whose mother tongue is not Dutch. It's not the same as regular Dutch class at school. In NT2 lessons the teacher takes your background, your mother tongue and the specific challenges of learning Dutch as a new language into account.

Read more about Dutch as a second language if you want to know exactly what NT2 involves.

Who are NT2 lessons for?

  • Newcomers who need to integrate: you need NT2 lessons to pass your civic integration exam
  • Migrant workers: you want to function better at work and communicate with colleagues
  • Partners of Dutch people: you've moved to the Netherlands and want to learn the language
  • Expats: you live and work in the Netherlands and want to feel at home. Also read our article for expats in the Netherlands
  • International students: you're studying in the Netherlands and want to participate outside university too

Types of NT2 lessons

Civic integration lessons

Focused on the civic integration exam. You train all parts: reading, listening, writing and speaking. The teacher knows the exam and practises specifically with you.

Work Dutch

Focused on the workplace. You learn business language, writing emails, attending meetings and talking to clients.

Conversation lessons

Focused on speaking and understanding. You practise real conversations: at the doctor's, in the shop, with the neighbours.

General NT2 lessons

A broad approach. You work on all skills: reading, listening, speaking and writing. A good Dutch language course combines all these skills.

What to expect from NT2 lessons?

The first lesson

In the first lesson the teacher tests your level. How much Dutch do you already speak? Can you read? Write? Based on that she creates a lesson plan. This plan is your roadmap.

A typical lesson

A good lesson is varied. You start with a chat (warm-up). Then you work on a theme: doing groceries, introducing yourself, making an appointment. You practise speaking, reading and listening. At the end the teacher summarises what you've learned and gives you homework.

Progress and results

After a few weeks you'll already notice a difference. You understand more of conversations around you. You dare to speak Dutch more often. After 80 hours you reach A1 level. After 120 hours A2 level. Most students pass their civic integration exam within 40 weeks.

How to choose the right NT2 lessons?

Checklist for choosing NT2 lessons

  • Group size: the smaller the better. Maximum 6-8 people
  • Teacher experience: how many years? How many students guided?
  • Pass rate: how often do students pass the exam?
  • Flexibility: can you catch up on lessons? Different times available?
  • Materials: included or separate cost?
  • Trial lesson: can you meet first?
  • Reviews: what do other students say?

When choosing an NT2 course these points are equally important.
