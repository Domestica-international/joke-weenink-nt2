Course Comparison

Compare NT2 courses: private, group or online?

By Joke Weenink · NT2 teacher · 21+ years of experience

You want to learn Dutch. But which course do you choose? Private lessons, group lessons or an online course? They all have their advantages. In this article I compare the three options honestly. So you can make the right choice.

I'm Joke Weenink, NT2 teacher with over 21 years of experience. I've guided more than 1,000 students through all three formats. I know exactly what works and for whom.

The three NT2 course types

There are three popular ways to take a Dutch language course. Each has its own character, advantages and price tag.

1. Private lessons

€65 per hour

With private lessons you get one-on-one tuition from an NT2 teacher. Every lesson is tailor-made for you. Your level, your goals, your pace.

Advantages

  • Fastest way to learn
  • 100% personal attention
  • Flexible scheduling
  • Custom lesson plan
  • Works on your weak points

Disadvantages

  • Higher price per hour
  • Less practice with others
  • You need to stay self-motivated

Ideal for

Busy professionals, people with a deadline (exam date), students who want fast results or need extra attention on specific skills.

2. Group lessons

€25 per hour per person

In group lessons you learn together with other students. The group is small: maximum 6 people. Everyone is at the same level. You practise a lot together.

Advantages

  • Affordable
  • Lots of speaking practice
  • Social and motivating
  • Fixed structure and rhythm
  • You also learn from classmates

Disadvantages

  • Less personal attention
  • Fixed lesson times
  • Pace set by the group

Ideal for

People who like learning together, students who want to practise speaking, and anyone looking for an affordable option with social contacts.

3. Online course

€450 for 10 weeks

The online course consists of weekly lessons via video call. You get 1 hour of tuition per week, for 10 weeks. You can join from home or the office.

Advantages

  • Learn from anywhere
  • No travel time
  • Flexible and modern
  • All materials included
  • Ideal for busy schedules

Disadvantages

  • Less direct contact
  • Requires self-discipline
  • Technology needs to work

Ideal for

Expats who travel a lot, people outside the Randstad, students with changing schedules and anyone who prefers learning from home.

Decision guide: which course suits you?

Still unsure? Use this guide. Read the situations and see which fits best.

Choose private if...

  • You want fast results
  • You have an exam date approaching
  • Your schedule keeps changing
  • You need extra help on specific parts
  • You prefer one-on-one learning

Choose group if...

  • You enjoy learning with others
  • You want lots of speaking practice
  • You like a fixed rhythm
  • Budget is important to you
  • You also want social contacts

Choose online if...

  • You're often on the road
  • You live far from a school
  • You prefer learning from home
  • You need a flexible solution
  • You're good at self-discipline

Many students also combine formats. They start with private lessons to quickly build the basics. Then switch to group lessons for more conversation practice. There's no wrong choice. Only the choice that best fits your life.

How long does an NT2 course take?

Average duration

You reach A1 level in about 80 lesson hours. For A2 (the integration level) you need around 120 lesson hours. Most students pass within 40 weeks. Read more about the levels in our article on Dutch as a second language.

With private lessons it often goes faster. You don't spend time waiting for others. Every minute is for you. With group lessons the pace is slightly slower, but you practise more speaking.
