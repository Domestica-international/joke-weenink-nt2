NT2 Teacher

What does an NT2 teacher do? And why does it make such a difference?

By Joke Weenink · NT2 teacher since 2004

You want to learn Dutch. Maybe for your civic integration, maybe for work, maybe because you want to feel at home in the Netherlands. Whatever your reason: a good NT2 teacher makes the difference between struggling and enjoying the journey.

But what does an NT2 teacher actually do? And how do you recognise a good one? In this article I explain everything. As a teacher who has been giving NT2 lessons for over 21 years, I'm happy to share what I've learned.

What is an NT2 teacher?

NT2 stands for Dutch as a Second Language. An NT2 teacher is a teacher who teaches Dutch to people who don't speak the language as their mother tongue. That sounds simple, but it's very different from regular Dutch classes.

An NT2 teacher understands what it's like to learn a new language. She knows the stumbling blocks. She knows that the "g" sound is difficult for some students. That the difference between "de" and "het" isn't logical. And that grammar rules only work when you practise them in real life.

What does an NT2 teacher actually do?

A good NT2 teacher does much more than just give lessons. These are the most important tasks:

1. Create a lesson plan at your level

Every student starts at a different point. Some already speak a little Dutch. Others start from zero. An NT2 teacher first tests your level. Then she creates a lesson plan that fits your situation. Not a standard programme, but a plan for you.

2. Train the four skills

Learning Dutch involves four things: reading, listening, speaking and writing. A good NT2 teacher trains them all. Not just from a book, but with real situations. Practising a conversation at the doctor's. Writing a letter for school. Understanding a news item.

3. Prepare for the exam

Many students need a civic integration exam. An NT2 teacher knows the exam inside out. She knows which parts are hardest. She practises specifically with you, so you're well prepared.

4. Motivate and guide

Learning a new language isn't always easy. There are days when you feel like you're not learning anything. A good teacher sees those moments. She encourages you. She shows you how much you've already achieved. That's at least as important as the lesson itself.

How do you recognise a good NT2 teacher?

Qualities of a good NT2 teacher

  • Patience: she repeats things without irritation
  • Flexibility: she adapts the lesson to your pace
  • Experience: she has proven results
  • Warmth: you feel comfortable
  • Clarity: she explains things simply
  • Structure: every lesson builds on the previous one
  • High pass rates: her students pass

These are exactly the qualities you're looking for when you start learning Dutch. A teacher who combines these things gives you the best chance of success.

Why does personal attention make such a difference?

In a large class with 20 students you might get 5 minutes of attention per lesson. Your questions are left unanswered. Your mistakes aren't corrected. You learn at the group's pace, not yours.

With a teacher who gives personal attention, everything is different. She hears which mistakes you make. She corrects you immediately. She adjusts the lesson if something is too difficult or too easy. This means you learn faster and remember more.

This applies especially to private lessons, but you also notice the difference in small groups. A group of maximum 6 people is ideal: you learn from each other, but still get enough attention.
