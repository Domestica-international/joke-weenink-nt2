Learning Dutch as an expat: why it changes your life in the Netherlands

You live in the Netherlands. Your work is in English. Your friends speak English. Why would you learn Dutch? Because it changes everything. This article explains how.

The English-speaking bubble

The Netherlands is one of the best countries in the world to live as an expat. Almost everyone speaks English. You can do groceries, visit the doctor and file your taxes without a word of Dutch. That's nice. At first.

But after a while you notice something. You miss things. Jokes you don't understand. Conversations at the school gate you can't join. Emails from the municipality you can't read. Slowly a feeling grows: I live here, but I don't really belong.

That feeling doesn't have to be there. Learning Dutch is the key to truly feeling at home in the Netherlands.

Five reasons why expats should learn Dutch

1. Your career gets a boost

Yes, many companies work in English. But not all. And even in English-speaking companies, informal conversations, lunches and meetings sometimes happen in Dutch. If you can join in, you build relationships faster. And relationships lead to promotions.

2. Your social life becomes richer

Dutch people are friendly. But forming real friendships is difficult if you don't speak the language. The key to Dutch friendships? Speaking Dutch. Even a little. The Dutch enormously appreciate it when you try.

3. You understand your children better

If your children go to school in the Netherlands, they learn Dutch fast. They talk to friends in Dutch. They sing Dutch songs. If you don't understand the language, you miss part of their world.

4. You feel at home

Feeling at home isn't just about having a house. It's about understanding what's happening around you. Understanding the radio. Getting a joke. Having a chat with your neighbour.

5. The road to the Dutch passport

If you want the Dutch passport, you need to integrate (inburgeren). That means: passing the civic integration exam at A2 level. The sooner you start, the easier it becomes.

The three excuses every expat makes

"Everyone speaks English anyway"

That's true. And that's exactly the problem. Because everyone speaks English, you never learn Dutch. You end up in a comfort zone you can't get out of. But that comfort zone is also a limitation.

"I don't have time"

You work full-time. You have children. The good news: you don't need to study three hours every day. With one hour per week in a good Dutch language course and 15 minutes per day of practice, you'll make serious progress.

With Joke you can plan flexibly. Evening lessons, weekend lessons, online lessons. There's always a time that works. Check the lesson options.

"Dutch is too difficult"

Dutch is actually one of the easiest languages for English speakers. The grammar is similar to English. Many words are recognisable. Most expats studying with Joke are surprised how quickly they progress. A2 level takes about 120 lesson hours. Most students pass within 40 weeks. And the pass rate? 98%.

Did you know?

Dutch is in the top 3 of easiest languages to learn for English speakers. It shares much vocabulary with English and German. Words like "water", "man", "hand" and "arm" are exactly the same. You already know more Dutch than you think.
